BEFORE YOUR VISIT

What to Do During a Dental Emergency

These temporary measures may help protect your mouth while you arrange appropriate professional care.

For a Toothache

Gently rinse with warm water. Use a cold compress on the outside of your cheek if there is swelling. Never place aspirin directly on your tooth or gums.

For a Broken Tooth

Rinse gently with warm water, save any broken fragments if possible, and avoid chewing on the affected side.

For a Knocked-Out Permanent Tooth

Hold the tooth by its crown, not its root. If dirty, rinse it gently without scrubbing. Keep it moist in milk or an appropriate tooth-preservation solution and seek urgent dental care immediately. Do not reinsert a baby tooth.

For Bleeding

Apply gentle, steady pressure with clean gauze. Seek emergency medical care if bleeding is heavy or will not stop.

COMMON QUESTIONS

Emergency Dental Care FAQs

Helpful answers to common questions about urgent dental problems.

What counts as a dental emergency?

Severe tooth pain, a knocked-out permanent tooth, a fractured tooth with significant pain, facial swelling, and uncontrolled oral bleeding can require urgent assessment. Breathing or swallowing difficulties require emergency medical care.

Should I wait if my toothache gets better?

Temporary improvement does not always mean the underlying problem has resolved. Arrange a dental assessment, particularly if pain returns or you develop swelling or fever.

Can a knocked-out tooth be saved?

A permanent tooth may sometimes be saved if treated quickly. Handle it by the crown, keep it moist, and seek urgent dental care immediately. Never reinsert a knocked-out baby tooth.

How do I request an emergency appointment?

Use our contact page to send an appointment request. Explain your symptoms clearly. If you have a life-threatening symptom, seek emergency medical assistance rather than waiting for a reply.
